Vayu View is an indoor air quality intelligence platform built at [Vayu Guard](https://www.vayuguard.com/). It takes live readings from air quality monitors installed across a client's buildings — particulate matter, TVOC, CO₂, humidity — and turns them into something a building administrator can act on.

I joined as the company's first and only designer and built this surface from nothing.

## Who this is for

The primary user is a **Building Administration Head** — the person responsible for the physical environment of a property. They are not analysts and not scientists. They manage buildings, respond to complaints, and answer to owners and tenants.

Our clients span real estate, IT campuses, hotels, residential complexes and commercial property. What they had in common was how little they could see.

## What they were doing before

Going into design, I spent a day on a commercial site with our CMO, walking the building and talking to the people who manage it. The goal was requirement gathering — understanding how air quality actually gets handled day to day, before anyone drew a screen.

What we found is that clients fell into three groups:

- **Handheld meters.** Someone physically walks to a location, takes a spot reading, and writes it down. One point in space, one moment in time.
- **Spreadsheets.** Readings get typed into a sheet. By the time anyone looks at the sheet, the air has changed.
- **Nothing at all.** Air quality is managed reactively — when somebody complains.

All three share the same failure: they tell you what the air *was* like, somewhere, at some point. None of them tell you what's wrong right now, or where.

## The hard part: one client is not one building

The design problem that took the longest to solve was **hierarchy**.

A single client is rarely a single space. A client is a portfolio of properties; each property has floors; each floor has zones; each zone has one or more monitors, and each monitor reports several pollutants continuously. Every one of those readings is live and every one of them can go bad independently.

That creates a genuine tension:

- Show everything, and the administrator drowns. Dozens of live values, all changing, none prioritised.
- Show a summary, and the administrator loses the detail they need to actually fix anything — knowing "this building is unhealthy" doesn't tell you which floor to send someone to.

The dashboard had to collapse a deep tree into a single glance without throwing away the path back down to the specific device that needs attention.

## How I approached it

I designed around a single question the administrator asks every morning: **"is anything wrong, and where?"**

Everything else on the dashboard is subordinate to answering that. Detail is available, but it is never the first thing you meet — you descend into it only after the dashboard has told you where to look.

## Outcome

Vayu View shipped and is in production. It now supports **15+ enterprise clients** across real estate, IT, hospitality and commercial property, and underpins **₹25L in platform revenue**.

The clearest signal that the information architecture was working came from support: **the volume of support tickets went down** after launch — clients were finding what they needed without asking us where it was.
