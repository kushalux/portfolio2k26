A Building Administration Head does not work at a desk. They walk floors, handle complaints, meet contractors, and deal with whatever the building throws at them that day. The [web dashboard](/case-studies/vayu-view-dashboard) assumes a user who has sat down to look at their portfolio. That assumption is wrong for most of their working day.

Mobile is where Vayu View meets them where they actually are.

## Mobile is not a smaller dashboard

The easy mistake — and the one I wanted to avoid — is treating mobile as the dashboard with things removed. That produces an app that does everything badly.

The two surfaces are used in genuinely different moments:

| | Web dashboard | Mobile app |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Where** | At a desk | Walking the building |
| **Why they opened it** | To review and investigate | Because something pinged them |
| **Session length** | Minutes | Seconds |
| **What they need** | Depth, comparison, history | A verdict and a location |

So mobile isn't a subset of the dashboard's features — it's a different job. The dashboard answers *"how are my buildings doing?"* Mobile answers *"something just happened — do I need to walk somewhere?"*

## Built around alerts, not browsing

Because the mobile session usually starts with a notification rather than a deliberate decision to check, the alert is the real entry point — not the home screen. The design has to carry the user from a push notification to *"here is what's wrong, here is where, here is whether it's getting worse"* without making them navigate the hierarchy by hand.

The hierarchy problem from the dashboard shows up here too, but inverted. On the dashboard the challenge is presenting a whole portfolio at once. On mobile the challenge is jumping straight to one node deep in that tree and still giving the user enough context to know where they are.
